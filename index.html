01The fragment

What Sermorelin actually is

Sermorelin is the minimal working version of the brain's growth-hormone signal: 29 amino acids, nothing extra — and the only molecule here that was ever a real, approved medicine.

29 of 44 amino acids carry the full signal. Sermorelin is the natural hormone, trimmed to its essence.

GHRH, minimal edition

Sermorelin is GHRH(1-29): the first 29 amino acids of the brain's 44-amino-acid growth-hormone-releasing hormone. Researchers found this fragment keeps full biological activity — the rest is optional.

A real prescription history

Sold as Geref, sermorelin acetate was FDA-approved for diagnosing and treating growth-hormone deficiency in children. No other peptide on this site can say that.

Discontinued, then resurrected

The manufacturer stopped making Geref in 2008 for business reasons, not safety ones. It now lives on via compounding pharmacies and wellness clinics — a very different regulatory universe.

Plain English

Most peptides here are lab rumors with good chemistry. Sermorelin is the opposite: a retired professional. It had a real medical career, got laid off when the business model died, and now does gig work at wellness clinics. The résumé is genuine — the current job description is not.

02The Geref years

From pharmacy shelf to gray zone

Sermorelin's arc is unique: approved medicine, quiet discontinuation, then a second life in the wellness industry built on a fraction of the original evidence.

Approved, discontinued, revived: the only prescription arc in this series.
Approved for children

Geref's approval covered pediatric growth-hormone deficiency — a specific, serious medical condition with clear diagnostic criteria. That evidence does not transfer to healthy adults.

Discontinued for business

The 2008 discontinuation was not a safety withdrawal. Manufacturing economics and newer alternatives ended it — an important distinction the forums usually get right.

The clinic revival

Wellness clinics now prescribe compounded sermorelin for aging and vitality — uses with far thinner evidence than the pediatric indication ever had.

Plain English

Here's the honest version: sermorelin earned its medical license treating sick kids, then lost its manufacturer. Its current career — wellness clinics, anti-aging promises — runs on the old license's reputation, not on new evidence. Credentials don't transfer between jobs.

04Evidence check

What the science actually supports

This is the strongest evidence file in the series — and it still doesn't cover what clinics sell it for. The gap between 'approved then' and 'claimed now' is the whole story.

Pediatric use: real evidence

Geref's approval rested on actual clinical trials in children with GH deficiency. This is regulatory-grade evidence — the only such file in this series.

Adult wellness use: thin

Small studies in adults exist, but nothing like the pediatric dossier. The anti-aging and vitality claims run well ahead of the data.

Compounded ≠ Geref

Today's clinic sermorelin is compounded, not manufactured — different quality oversight, different product, borrowing the old brand's credibility.

The approved use is well-evidenced; the current use is not. Same molecule, different evidence planets.
Plain English

Sermorelin is two stories in one bottle: a retired medicine with a solid pediatric file, and a wellness product with a thin adult file. Quoting the first story to sell the second is the core sleight of hand — and now you can see it.

06Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

The best-characterized safety profile in this series — for children, decades ago. For today's adult wellness use, the file is much thinner.

Banned in sport

Observed fact: WADA prohibits GHRH analogues including sermorelin (S2 category). The prescription history doesn't exempt it — athletes have been sanctioned.

Known short-term effects

Observed in trials: flushing, headache, dizziness, and injection-site reactions. The pediatric safety file is genuinely informative — a rarity here.

Compounded quality variance

Plausible: compounded products lack manufactured-drug oversight. Potency and purity can vary between pharmacies — 'same molecule' isn't 'same product.'

Adult metabolic effects

Theoretical: repeated GH pulses in healthy adults could affect glucose handling and tissue growth over time — unquantified for this use pattern.

Long-term adult outcomes

Unknown: no long-term studies of healthy adults using sermorelin for aging or vitality. The pediatric file doesn't answer adult questions.

Interactions

Unknown: limited interaction data; no data in pregnancy, no data in people with cancer or diabetes using it for wellness. Blank labels.

Plain English

Known: a real pediatric safety file, real short-term side effects, and a sport ban that ignores the prescription past. Theoretical: compounded quality variance and adult metabolic drift. Unknown: decades of adult wellness use — the exact experiment now running.
